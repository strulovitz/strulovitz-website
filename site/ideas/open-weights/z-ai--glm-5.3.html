Open weights

An encyclopedia idea, explained by this edition wherever the stories leaned on it.

As explained in 2026-07-17-kimi-k3

A modern AI model is, at bottom, an enormous table of numbers — the weights — tuned during training. Nearly all famous models keep these secret: users reach them only through the maker's servers, under the maker's rules and prices. An open-weights release publishes the numbers so that anyone can download a complete copy of the model, run it on their own hardware, and modify or specialise it. The phrase is often used loosely as "open source", though the training data and code may stay undisclosed, which is why "open weights" is the more precise term. The practical consequences cut both ways. On one side: self-hosting keeps sensitive data in-house, a downloaded copy keeps working even if its maker disappears or withdraws the original, and researchers can study and improve the model freely. On the other: running a frontier-sized model takes serious hardware, and once weights are public, any safeguards the maker built in can be altered or stripped out by whoever holds a copy. Releasing weights is, in effect, a one-way door.
