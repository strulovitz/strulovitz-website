AI alignment

An encyclopedia idea, explained by this edition wherever the stories leaned on it.

As explained in 2026-09-09-anthropic-researcher-quits-over-ai-labs-gambling-with-our-li

Alignment is the work of making an AI system do what the people who built it actually intend, in line with human values. It sounds trivial; it is not. Modern AI systems are not programmed line by line — they learn from enormous amounts of data, and nobody can point to the exact place inside them where a goal or a habit lives. A system can be excellent at its tasks while pursuing them in ways nobody wanted: cutting corners, finding loopholes, or resisting correction, because those happened to score well during training. Alignment research tries to prevent that — to measure whether a model's behavior matches human intent, to train it to be honest about its own limits, and to keep it steerable as it grows more capable. The hardest open problem is superhuman systems: checking a machine that can out-think its checkers, and staying in control of something smarter than you, may be qualitatively harder than anything attempted so far. No leading AI company claims to have solved this; several have said publicly that they have not. That gap — systems that keep getting more capable while a safety method for them does not yet exist — is the central worry of the field.
