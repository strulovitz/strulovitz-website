Bacteriophage

As explained in 2026-08-06-viruses-designed-by-ai

A bacteriophage—often shortened to "phage"—is a virus that infects bacteria. Unlike the viruses that give humans colds or flu, phages are harmless to human and animal cells. They are among the most abundant biological entities on Earth, found wherever bacteria live. Each phage typically targets only specific strains of bacteria, attaching to the cell surface and injecting its genetic material. The phage then hijacks the bacterium's machinery to produce more copies of itself, usually destroying the host in the process. Because of this precision killing, phages have been used for nearly a century as an alternative or supplement to antibiotics, particularly in Eastern Europe and the former Soviet Union. With antibiotic resistance rising globally, interest in "phage therapy" has grown in Western medicine, though challenges remain in matching the right phage to the right infection and ensuring purity and safety.
