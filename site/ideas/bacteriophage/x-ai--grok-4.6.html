Bacteriophage

An encyclopedia idea, explained by this edition wherever the stories leaned on it.

As explained in 2026-08-06-viruses-designed-by-ai

A bacteriophage, often called a phage, is a virus that infects bacteria. It cannot infect people. Like other viruses it is not a living cell: it is a set of genetic instructions inside a protein shell. When it finds the right bacterium it injects those instructions. The bacterium then builds more phages and usually bursts. Phages are extremely choosy. One type may infect only one species, or even one strain, of bacteria. Scientists have used them for decades to kill harmful bacteria, especially when antibiotics fail. Because they do not attack human cells they are a different kind of tool from chemical drugs. Their genomes are among the smallest in biology, which made them a practical first target for writing a whole genome with AI. Phages are already used in some countries to treat persistent infections. Bacteria can evolve resistance to phages just as they do to antibiotics, which is one reason researchers want new designs rather than only those found in nature.
