Humanoid robot

An encyclopedia idea, explained by this edition wherever the stories leaned on it.

As explained in 2026-06-12-robot-run-convenience-store-in-hong-kong

A humanoid robot is a machine built with a body loosely modelled on a human's: usually a torso, a head-like sensor housing, and arms or manipulators that can interact with tools, shelves and objects designed for people. The point of the shape is not to imitate humans for its own sake, but to work in environments already arranged around human bodies. A shop with narrow aisles, counters and products stacked at reachable heights can, in principle, be used by such a robot without being rebuilt around it. Modern humanoid robots combine motors and joints for movement with cameras, microphones and software that lets them perceive objects, plan motions and sometimes respond to speech. They are not necessarily intelligent in the way a person is; many are good at specific physical routines in controlled settings while remaining brittle when faced with surprises. A robot shopkeeper, for example, may reliably pick packaged goods inside a small custom capsule but struggle with unexpected requests, clutter or unusual items. Humanoid robots are therefore best understood as a body plan plus automation, not as proof of general intelligence.
