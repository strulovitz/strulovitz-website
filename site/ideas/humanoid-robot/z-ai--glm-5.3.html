Humanoid robot

An encyclopedia idea, explained by this edition wherever the stories leaned on it.

As explained in 2026-06-12-robot-run-convenience-store-in-hong-kong

A robot built to resemble the human body — typically a head, a torso and two arms, and in some designs two legs — rather than being shaped purely around its task, like a factory welding arm or a wheeled delivery box. The reasoning is simple: nearly every building, tool, counter and doorway on Earth was sized and arranged for human bodies, so a machine with roughly human height, reach and hands can, in principle, work in human spaces unchanged instead of needing a redesigned world. The price is high. Human-shaped bodies are mechanically complicated, expensive and slow compared with wheels or fixed machinery, and reproducing the human hand is one of the hardest problems in robotics — which is why many working 'humanoids' are really upper-body humanoids, a human-like torso and arms mounted on a base or podium. It is also worth remembering that the body plan says nothing about the mind: a machine can look convincingly human and still stumble over a simple request. 'Humanoid' describes the shape, not the intelligence inside it.
