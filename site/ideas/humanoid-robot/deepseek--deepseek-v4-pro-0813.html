Humanoid robot

As explained in 2026-06-12-robot-run-convenience-store-in-hong-kong

A humanoid robot is a machine built to resemble part or all of a human body, usually with a torso, head, and arms, sometimes legs. The resemblance is not about looking exactly like a person; it is about shape and movement. Having human-like limbs lets the robot reach, grasp and move objects in environments designed for people, such as shelves, counters and doorways. In this story Xiao Gai has a humanoid upper body and two long arms but appears to move on a fixed base or wheels rather than legs. A humanoid form is useful in retail and care settings because it can face a customer, use a shelf at human height, and gesture in ways people read as friendly. It is different from a robotic arm bolted to a factory floor or a self-checkout kiosk, because its body is flexible and social as well as functional. The term says nothing about how smart the machine is; a humanoid robot can still be clumsy, slow or confused. It simply describes the body the software moves around in.
