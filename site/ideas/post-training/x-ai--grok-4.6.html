Post-training

An encyclopedia idea, explained by this edition wherever the stories leaned on it.

As explained in 2026-08-13-grok-4-6

A large model is first pretrained: it is shown a huge amount of text and code and learns to predict the next token. That base is fluent but not yet a product. Post-training is everything that happens after — extra passes that teach the model to follow instructions, reason step by step, write working software, and refuse certain requests. Typical stages include supervised fine-tuning, where the model imitates carefully chosen examples, and reinforcement learning, where it is rewarded for answers that graders or other models prefer. Labs often run a “supplemental” pass that injects a new kind of data, such as engineering traces or company documents, without starting pretraining over. Newer models are also used to write the training examples for the next one. That loop is not a fully self-improving system, but it is how a modest upgrade, given more of the right data, can feel like a different model.
