Genome language model

An encyclopedia idea, explained by this edition wherever the stories leaned on it.

As explained in 2026-08-06-viruses-designed-by-ai

A genome language model is an AI system trained on genetic sequences rather than ordinary writing. DNA and related genetic material can be represented as long strings made from a small alphabet of chemical units. By seeing many examples, a model can learn which patterns tend to occur together and which changes are compatible with the sequences in its training data. Like a text model, it can then propose a continuation or generate a new sequence under chosen conditions. But it does not directly prove that its output will work in a cell. Biological function depends on many interacting processes: a sequence must be made, introduced into an appropriate experimental system and tested. Genome language models are therefore tools for proposing candidates, not substitutes for laboratory evidence. Their usefulness depends on training data, design goals, experimental validation and safety controls.
