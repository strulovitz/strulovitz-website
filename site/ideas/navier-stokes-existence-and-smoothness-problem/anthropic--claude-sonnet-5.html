Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem

An encyclopedia idea, explained by this edition wherever the stories leaned on it.

As explained in 2026-09-08-openai-claims-to-have-solved-the-navier-stokes-problem

The Navier-Stokes equations are the standard mathematical description of how fluids such as air and water move, widely used in engineering and weather modeling despite being poorly understood at a theoretical level. The Millennium Prize version of the problem asks whether, starting from smooth and well-behaved initial conditions, solutions to these equations always stay smooth forever, or whether they can 'blow up' — develop a point where a quantity like fluid speed becomes infinite in a finite amount of time. Answering this would settle a deep question about whether the equations themselves are a complete and reliable description of fluid behavior, including turbulence, which remains one of the least understood phenomena in physics. A full solution requires addressing several related statements about different versions of the problem (forced versus unforced, and other variants), which is why a partial result can resolve some of these statements while leaving others open.
