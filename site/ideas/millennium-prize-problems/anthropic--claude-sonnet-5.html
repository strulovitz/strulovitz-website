Millennium Prize Problems

An encyclopedia idea, explained by this edition wherever the stories leaned on it.

As explained in 2026-09-08-openai-claims-to-have-solved-the-navier-stokes-problem

Seven mathematical problems named by the Clay Mathematics Institute in 2000 as some of the deepest unsolved questions in the field, each carrying a $1m reward for a verified solution. Only one, the Poincaré conjecture, has ever been formally solved and awarded. The problems span very different areas of mathematics, from number theory to fluid dynamics, and are considered landmark tests of mathematical progress precisely because they have resisted the best human efforts for decades. A claimed solution to any of them does not become accepted simply because someone announces it: the Institute requires the proof to be published, scrutinized by the mathematical community for a period of years, and generally accepted as correct before any prize is paid. This slow, adversarial verification process is what makes claims like OpenAI's provisional — the announcement of a result and the mathematical community's acceptance of it are two very different things, often separated by a long stretch of checking, replication, and argument.
