Data regurgitation

An encyclopedia idea, explained by this edition wherever the stories leaned on it.

As explained in 2026-09-08-openai-claims-to-have-solved-the-navier-stokes-problem

Data regurgitation describes a risk with AI models trained on material submitted by users: rather than only learning general patterns, a model can absorb and later reproduce specific content it was exposed to during training, sometimes closely enough to resemble the original. This becomes a concern when users share unpublished or sensitive work with an AI tool, such as code, drafts, or research notes, and the company operating that tool reserves the right to use those interactions for further training, typically unless the user opts out. If a later, more capable model is trained on that data, it can potentially produce results that echo the original unpublished work without anyone directly copying it, making it difficult to distinguish independent discovery from indirect leakage. Because the underlying training process is not fully transparent even to the companies running it, claims of regurgitation are hard to prove or disprove definitively, and disputes over it usually come down to competing accounts of timing, approach, and what data was technically accessible.
