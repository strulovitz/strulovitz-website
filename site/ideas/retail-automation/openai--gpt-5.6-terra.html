Retail automation

As explained in 2026-06-12-robot-run-convenience-store-in-hong-kong

Retail automation is the use of machines and software to perform work involved in selling goods. It can include self-checkout systems, electronic inventory records, warehouse sorting equipment, vending machines and robots that stock shelves or hand products to customers. The aim is not always to remove every worker. Automation may handle repetitive tasks, extend opening hours, speed up a particular step or provide a new kind of service. Whether it works depends on more than the machine’s headline abilities. A retailer must consider breakdowns, restocking, security, payment problems, accessibility, customer confusion and rare events that a scripted system may not handle. A useful question is therefore not simply whether a machine can complete a demonstration. It is whether it can do the work reliably enough, often enough and safely enough in the varied conditions of an operating shop. Public-facing systems also have a social dimension: customers need to know how to use them and what happens when they need help.
