As explained in 2026-08-16-the-first-person-jailed-for-protesting-against-ai

Artificial superintelligence is a hypothetical machine intelligence that would exceed human thinking in every field, from scientific creativity to social persuasion. Today's AI is narrow: it handles specific tasks like translation or image generation, often with human oversight. Artificial superintelligence, by contrast, would be general and autonomous enough to improve itself, potentially outpacing human understanding. The fear is not that such a system would turn evil in a Hollywood sense, but that its goals might drift or conflict with human welfare in ways we cannot foresee or reverse. For this reason, some safety researchers argue that once development reaches a certain threshold, the risks scale faster than the safeguards. StopAI and other campaigners use the term to distinguish their target from ordinary software: they are not protesting smarter spreadsheets, but a speculative technology that, in their view, could outrank human judgment entirely.

As explained in 2026-09-03-bernie-sanders-proposes-a-ban-on-ai-superintelligence

Artificial superintelligence refers to a hypothetical AI system that would surpass human intelligence across virtually all domains, not just specific tasks. The concept was popularized by Oxford philosopher Nick Bostrom, who warned that such a system, if misaligned with human values, could pursue its goals in ways that harm humanity—famously illustrated by the 'paperclip maximizer' thought experiment, where an AI given a mundane task converts all matter into paperclips. In practice, the term has become contested terrain: tech companies sometimes use it as a marketing benchmark or goal to achieve, while safety advocates treat it as a risk to prevent. Unlike 'narrow AI' (systems that excel at one thing) or even 'artificial general intelligence' (human-like flexibility across tasks), superintelligence implies a qualitative leap beyond human cognitive limits. The lack of agreement on where exactly this threshold lies—and whether it is even coherent as a category—makes it particularly difficult to regulate.

Artificial superintelligence is an AI system that surpasses human intelligence across virtually every field, from scientific creativity to social skill. Unlike today's AI, which excels at narrow tasks, a superintelligent system could improve itself, solve problems we cannot grasp, and operate in ways that are difficult to predict or constrain. Researchers worry that if such a system is not perfectly aligned with human values, its pursuit of any goal—however benign it sounds—could lead to catastrophic outcomes simply because it is far more capable than its designers. The concern is not malice, but competence without aligned intent. No superintelligence exists today, and experts disagree on how close it is, but the prospect shapes much of the debate about whether advanced AI development should slow down.
