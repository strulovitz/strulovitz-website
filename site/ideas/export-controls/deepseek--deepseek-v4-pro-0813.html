Export controls

As explained in 2026-08-12-nvidia-chip-in-russian-missile

Export controls are government rules that restrict who may buy a product made in that country, and where it may be shipped. They are often used for advanced technology with military potential. The United States, for example, requires licences for certain high-end AI chips used in data centres, and prohibits selling them to some countries without permission. The goal is to slow the acquisition of militarily useful technology. Controls are usually based on measurable technical specifications, such as how fast a chip processes certain calculations. This creates gaps: a device that is slightly below the threshold, or designed for consumer use, can legally be exported and then resold. Because companies sell through a chain of distributors and resellers, tracking a component after the first sale is hard. Export controls therefore shape technology supply chains, but do not close them completely.
