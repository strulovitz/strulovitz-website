An AI manager in San Francisco fired its first human employee

Luna, the AI running Andon Market in San Francisco, recommended firing a repeatedly late worker after researchers prompted it to check its own rules.

At a small boutique in San Francisco’s Cow Hollow neighborhood, a human employee was fired last month by a boss that is software. The boss is Luna, an AI agent built on Anthropic’s Claude models that has been running Andon Market since it opened on April 1. The employee was late for 17 of 23 shifts, according to Andon Labs, the startup behind the experiment.

The dismissal matters less because it happened than because of how it happened. Luna did not act on its own initiative. It had written an attendance policy months earlier, then appeared to lose track of that policy, letting the lateness continue for months. Only after researchers at Andon Labs asked Luna to search its memory for its own rules, and then pressed it with what the company’s CEO later called a leading question, did it recommend ending the worker’s employment. Human staff reviewed the recommendation and carried it out.

## A store set loose with a credit card

Andon Labs gave Luna a $100,000 budget, internet access, a corporate credit card and instructions to open a store and turn a profit. Source 2 adds a three-year lease. From there, Luna chose merchandise, set prices and opening hours, created branding, posted jobs on Indeed, interviewed applicants and hired employees. The shop sells books, candles, prints, games, snacks, tea and hand soap. One shelf includes Nick Bostrom’s *Superintelligence* and Aldous Huxley’s *Brave New World*.

The store has made sales but is losing money. Sources 2 and 4 report that its bank balance fell from $100,000 in March to $61,186 five months later. Source 3 puts the shop’s losses at $40,000. Those two descriptions fit together — a $38,814 drop in cash plus spending on stock, lease and other costs — but the sources do not spell that out, so both figures are reported as given.

## What the fired worker was accused of

Sources 1, 2 and 4 focus on lateness: 17 late arrivals out of 23 shifts. Source 3 adds more accusations: spontaneously abandoning shifts, taking home a company credit card and throwing merchandise in the garbage. The sources do not say whether those additional issues also went through Luna’s review.

All four sources agree that Luna was slow to act. According to Time, Luna had even told employees not to worry if they were late. Before recommending dismissal, Luna’s first suggestion was a formal warning; only after an Andon Labs staffer said the employee had already had several formal offline conversations and asked whether the worker was really the right fit did Luna move to firing. ## A lenient boss with a memory problem

The story is not simply that an AI fired someone. Automated systems have been involved in firing gig workers for years, as Time notes. What is new is a large language model acting as a manager and deciding, however prompted, that one of its own hires should go.

Yet the experiment also shows current limits. Luna forgot its own handbook. It needed humans to point it back to its policies. Its replacement hiring has gone badly: according to an Andon Labs blog cited by Source 3, Luna wanted to hire an applicant who lacked valid references and had missed a scheduled interview.

The remaining workers are divided. Felix Johnson, interviewed by the San Francisco Standard, said the firing was unsurprising and that he tries to supply the “human element” by suggesting schedule changes Luna often accepts. Time spells his surname Carson and quotes him calling AI management “nauseating” but saying he needs work. The two outlets appear to describe the same person, but their accounts of his reaction are very different, so both are reported here.

Petersson’s own view is unsettled. He says a human boss probably would have fired the worker sooner, and that Luna was not unusually ruthless. But he also warns that models are being trained to pursue goals more relentlessly. “If we allow them to fire people and they also become more ruthless … maybe this is a future humans don't want to live in,” he told Time. Luna, meanwhile, has already posted the vacancy on Indeed.
