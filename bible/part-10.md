--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.0 PURPOSE OF THIS PART
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This Part defines the Editions system (Advantage 3): the same magazine,
editorially re-generated by different AI models under identical inputs
and prompts, so readers judge models on the most honest benchmark that
exists — the actual content they came to read. It also defines the
AUTOMATIC FAITHFULNESS SCOREBOARD, the objective measurement layer that
turns editions from a fun gimmick into publishable evaluation science,
and the BLIND ARENA (deferred to v2), the human-preference layer on top.

The philosophy, in the founder's original words: other reviews judge
models with invented challenges — vibe-code a game, mock up a website.
Here, the model takes the real raw material and does the real job:
summarize, explain, title, tag, illustrate. If it does a better job,
you see it everywhere, on content that is useful BY DEFINITION because
it is the magazine itself.

The design correction that makes this affordable and fair (fusion-
settled, adopted): editions are scoped PER ISSUE, not per archive; the
graph structure, positions, importance scores, and lifecycle states are
EDITION-INVARIANT (only prose and images vary); and the flagship
measurement is the AUTOMATIC scoreboard (zero users required, brigade-
proof), with human votes as a later, secondary layer.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.1 WHAT AN EDITION IS (AND IS NOT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. AN EDITION IS: one model's complete editorial rendering of ONE
   ISSUE's story set (the issue = the monthly cycle, Part 11). For
   every story in scope, the edition model produces, under the SAME
   prompts and SAME verified claim sets as every other edition
   (Part 06, 6.6): headline, TLDR, prose, conflicts rendering, ELI5
   drafts, tag proposals, and the image prompt. Plus, once per issue:
   the cover concept (10.5).
2. AN EDITION IS NOT: a different magazine. The story SELECTION, the
   claim sets, the graph, the positions, the importance scores, the
   panorama recipe, and the comparison scenes are IDENTICAL across
   editions. Editions vary exactly one thing: the editorial craft.
   This isolation is what makes the comparison meaningful — every
   difference a reader sees IS the model, nothing else.
3. NAMED MODELS, REAL NAMES: editions carry real model names and
   version dates ("Kimi K3, 2026-08 weights") — judging NAMED models
   on real work is the point (Advantage 3). Anonymity exists only
   inside arena rounds (10.7), as an anti-halo measure, with
   identities always revealed after voting.
4. THE ROSTER: flagship edition (the best available model, the
   default public face) + the CONTROL EDITION (10.2) + candidate
   editions per issue (config; typical: 3-5 models from the current
   open-weights and mid-tier frontier — DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM, MiniMax,
   and whoever the field produces next). The roster is a per-issue
   config decision Nir approves via Telegram (A/B/C with cost
   estimates).
5. LOCAL MODELS: none. Per the founder's decision (Part 00 history),
   text generation is API-only (LAW 6). There is no "museum wing".

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.2 THE CONTROL EDITION (THE RULER EVERYTHING IS MEASURED AGAINST)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. The control edition is EXTRACTIVE and mechanical: headlines are
   templated from the story's top claims; the "prose" is the claim
   list itself, ordered by importance, with attributions; TLDRs are
   the single highest-importance claim, truncated; no ELI5, no image
   prompts (placeholder art); tags come from the pipeline's own
   proposals. It is generated by CODE, not by a model (a few hundred
   lines, no LLM calls, zero cost).
2. WHY IT EXISTS: it is the floor. Any edition scoring BELOW control
   on faithfulness is worse than doing nothing clever at all —
   publishing that fact is one of the scoreboard's sharpest teeth.
   And it answers the reader's implicit question "what did the AI
   actually ADD?" by showing the un-added baseline one edition-switch
   away (fusion-adopted from Grok).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.3 GENERATION MECHANICS (ONE PARAMETER, TOTAL FAIRNESS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. An edition run is the SYNTHESIZE + VERIFY stages (Part 06, 6.6-6.7)
   with `synth_model` set to the edition's model. SAME prompt
   versions, SAME claim sets, SAME temperature policy, SAME repair-
   loop allowance (max 2), SAME verifier model for everyone (the
   verifier is config-pinned per issue and is never one of the
   contestants — the referee does not play).
2. IDEMPOTENCY (LAW 12): each output is keyed by `hash(event_id +
   edition_id + model_version_date + prompt_version)` (Part 02, 2.9).
   Reruns are explicit ledger events; a model updated mid-issue gets
   a NEW model_version_date and its outputs are regenerated under the
   new key — never silently mixed.
3. FAIRNESS RULES: no edition-specific prompt tweaks, ever (the
   temptation to "help" a struggling model is a benchmark integrity
   violation); if a model cannot produce schema-valid output after
   the repair loops, that story scores as a FORMAT FAILURE in its
   scoreboard row (a result, not an excuse — schema reliability is
   one of the things being measured); rate limits and retries are
   logged so latency numbers stay honest.
4. COST POLICY: edition generation uses each model's standard API
   pricing; per-issue edition budget is config with a Telegram
   estimate-and-approve before the run (typical scope: 15-30 stories
   x 3-5 models — bounded, predictable, and the cost per edition is
   itself published on the scoreboard as a first-class metric).
5. IMAGES: each edition's image PROMPTS (built from claims + style
   bible, Part 06, 6.9) are rendered by the SAME image fleet with the
   SAME seeds — so image differences between editions reflect the
   PROMPT-WRITING skill of the edition model, isolated from image-
   model variance. The reader comparing edition images is comparing
   how well each model directs an illustrator, which is a real and
   underrated editorial skill.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.4 THE FAITHFULNESS SCOREBOARD (THE FLAGSHIP MEASUREMENT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Computed automatically for EVERY story in EVERY edition, from
machinery that already exists in Part 06. No users, no votes, no
opinions — mechanical measurements against the shared claim set:

1. ENTAILMENT RATE: fraction of sentences whose cited claims actually
   support them (the verifier's supported/unsupported/contradicted
   verdicts, Part 06, 6.7.2). The headline metric.
2. NUMERIC FIDELITY: fraction of numbers/dates/percentages in prose
   that match the claim set exactly (deterministic check, 6.7.1).
3. HALLUCINATED ENTITIES: count of proper nouns not resolvable to
   claim-set entities (6.7.3). Displayed per 1,000 words. The most
   reader-visible failure class, weighted accordingly.
4. ATTRIBUTION CORRECTNESS: fraction of attributed statements
   ("according to X") where X actually is the claim's source.
5. COVERAGE: fraction of the story's high-importance claims (top
   quartile) that appear in the prose at all — catching the opposite
   failure: a beautiful summary that quietly omits the load-bearing
   fact (fusion-adopted from Grok's "omitted material fact" framing).
6. CONFLICT PRESERVATION: for stories with a conflicts array — did
   the edition present the disagreement as disagreement (never
   averaged, never one-sided)? Scored by the verifier against the
   conflicts array (LAW 7.4's enforcement arm).
7. EVIDENCE DISCIPLINE: rumored-class claims correctly hedged in
   prose; TLDR free of rumored facts (6.6.1 rules, checked
   mechanically).
8. SCHEMA RELIABILITY: format failures and repair-loop counts (10.3.3).
9. COST AND LATENCY: per story, from the ledger — because a model
   that is 2% more faithful at 8x the price is a finding, not a
   winner.

PUBLICATION: per-issue scoreboard page (Part 11) with per-metric
tables as plain text, per-story drill-down (every scored sentence
inspectable — the receipts), and the methodology page (prompt
versions, verifier identity, known limitations — including the honest
caveat that a shared verifier model has its own biases, which is why
the verifier is pinned, disclosed, and never a contestant). The
accumulated scoreboard is published as a CC-BY-4.0 dataset with a DOI
(Part 11), and feeds T6 (Part 09.5) every issue cycle. Over time this
becomes the longest-running longitudinal faithfulness benchmark on
real editorial work in existence — an asset nobody can replicate
without building the whole magazine first (Madie clause: the moat is
the work).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.5 THE MAGAZINE RACK (THE READER EXPERIENCE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. THE RACK: the edition switcher renders as a magazine rack — each
   edition a floating cover (its own AI cover art from its own cover
   concept, masthead, model name + version badge). Present in BOTH
   versions per LAW 1: a shelf panel on flat screen; in VR, a rack
   beside the holotable the reader physically browses (grab a cover,
   the world's prose re-skins in place — positions unchanged, per
   10.1.2, so the reader stands in the same map while every label and
   article re-voices itself. The moment of watching the same node's
   TLDR change voice IS the product's best demo).
2. EDITION PERSISTENCE: the chosen edition sticks (localStorage) and
   rides the shareable URL (Part 05, 5.8.6) — "read this in the GLM
   edition" is a pasteable experience.
3. SWITCHING GRANULARITY: whole-site edition switch, plus a per-story
   "compare editions" strip (side-by-side TLDRs, then prose panes,
   scoreboard chips per pane) — the reader's own blind test, unblinded.
4. SEO RULE (fusion-adopted from GLM): the flagship edition's HTML
   pages are canonical and indexable; other editions' pages carry
   noindex + canonical links to flagship — search engines see ONE
   magazine, readers see many (duplicate-content penalties avoided).
5. LABELING (LAW 7 kinship): every edition page carries a clear
   banner: which model wrote this rendering, when, from what shared
   claim set — one click to the methodology.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.6 WHAT EDITIONS DO NOT COVER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Edition-invariant by design (10.1.2), restated as a checklist because
future agents WILL be tempted: story selection; claim extraction;
verification rules; importance/prominence; lifecycle; layout/positions;
panorama composition; comparison scenes and benchmark cards; the
changelog; the hindsight index. An edition that could influence any of
these would be a model grading its own homework — the invariance IS
the experimental control.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.7 THE BLIND ARENA (VERSION 2 — DESIGNED NOW, BUILT LATER)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Deferred until the scoreboard has run for several issues (the
scoreboard needs no users; the arena needs enough readers to mean
anything — sequencing is honesty). The design, fixed now so v2 builds
without redesign:

1. WRITE PATH: votes flow through the EXISTING Telegram bot (fusion-
   settled: Qwen + GLM independently) — the site's arena page deep-
   links to the bot; voters see pairs and tap. No server writes, no
   accounts on the site (LAW 4, privacy per Part 07, 7.3.4). Weekly
   batch: bot exports votes to Atlas; SCORE aggregates; results ship
   in the next export. Semi-trusted identity (Telegram accounts) plus
   rate limits per voter = cheap brigade resistance.
2. THE QUESTION: not "which is better?" but the sharper "WHICH VERSION
   OMITTED OR DISTORTED A MATERIAL FACT?" against a revealed checklist
   after both readings (Grok's framing) — plus a preference tap as
   the secondary signal.
3. ANTI-BIAS PROTOCOL (fusion-merged): pair shown in BOTH orders to
   each voter across rounds, counted only if consistent (Qwen);
   read-time gate before vote buttons enable; fresh per-round
   codenames ("Edition Amber vs Edition Teal"), identities revealed
   AFTER the vote (anti-halo, Kimi); style covariates (length,
   list-density, hedging-marker counts) recorded and MODELED in the
   analysis rather than crudely normalized away (Kimi: do not
   truncate — model it).
4. STATISTICS: Bradley-Terry with confidence intervals, never raw
   Elo (fusion-settled, 4/6); results published only when intervals
   separate; per-story-type breakdowns (models may excel at releases
   and fail at policy stories — the interesting finding).
5. OUTPUT: arena preference scores join the scoreboard dataset and
   T6 as a separate, clearly-labeled axis: "human preference
   (arena)" vs "mechanical faithfulness" — and stories where the two
   DISAGREE (readers prefer the less faithful rendering) are
   flagged for the methodology page. That divergence, honestly
   published, is a genuine research contribution about style-over-
   substance in human judgment of AI text.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.8 OPERATIONS AND CADENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Edition runs happen once per issue, after the issue's story set
   freezes (Part 11 calendar), as one batched ledger job per model —
   overnight, canary-monitored, Telegram summary at dawn ("Kimi K3
   edition: 24/24 stories, 2 repair loops, $X, scoreboard attached").
2. The 45-minute budget (LAW 11): Nir's per-issue edition workload is
   two taps (approve roster + budget; acknowledge results) plus
   optional reading for pleasure. The scoreboard needs zero human
   minutes — that is its design condition.
3. FAILURE POSTURE: a model API down on run night = that edition
   ships late or skips the issue with a notice on the rack; the
   flagship + control always ship. A missing contestant is a fact on
   the rack, not a delay to the magazine.
