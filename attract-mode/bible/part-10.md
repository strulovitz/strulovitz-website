10.5 THE MAKING OF
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ONE STUDIO, ONE PIECE. A Making Of shows how one finished thing
was made: a song, a picture, a clip, a voice.

HOW IT IS MADE.
1. THE PROOF PIECE. Nir makes the piece, with an agent's help if
   needed. The agent writes down every step that was really
   taken.
2. THE SHEET. "am art sheet NAME" turns those steps into one
   printed page (example: 10.3, A). The WORKHORSE writes it from
   the steps and from the studio's own Quick Guide.
3. THE DRY RUN. Nir follows the sheet once without recording.
   Where the sheet was wrong, it is corrected.
4. THE RECORDING. Nir records his screen and his voice, reading
   the sheet. He memorises nothing.
5. "am art in NAME": the ear finds CUT HERE and WE ARE BACK, and
   the script cuts what lies between. STEP ONE, STEP TWO become
   the chapters. The captions are made. The loudness is set.
6. THE UPLOAD: as in Part 06, section 6.13.

THE SHAPE OF THE FILM.
1. The finished piece comes first, with one sentence.
2. The steps.
3. One thing that went wrong or surprised Nir. It stays in.
4. No promise that it works on every PC.

ITS PAGE IN MOODLE. A Making Of is one section of its art
department, a free course like the others:
1. The film.
2. WHAT YOU NEED: the card on which it was proven, the disk
   space, the system.
3. THE WORDS: every prompt and setting that was used, as text
   to copy.
4. The link to the studio's own page.
5. One line: "Want to do this together? Co-op."

10.7 CO-OP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT IS. One hour by video. The customer shares their screen.
The work is done on the customer's PC, by the customer's hands
and the customer's own AI. Nir sits beside them: "we ask the AI
together". Nir never takes control of their PC.

THE CO-OP PAGE says: what it is; the menu; the price; the
promise; and what to write in the email (example: 10.3, B).

THE STEPS.
1. The customer writes to the contact address.
2. Nir pastes the email to the agent. "am coop reply" does the
   FIT CHECK and writes a draft reply. Nir sends it.
3. THE FIT CHECK has three answers.
   FITS: the customer's card is at least as large as a card on
   which Nir proved this studio.
   NO: the studio's own page states a minimum above the
   customer's card. The reply offers studios that fit.
   UNKNOWN: anything else. The reply says so honestly: "I have
   not run this on a card like yours. We can try. If it does not
   run, you pay nothing."
4. A reply to FITS holds: one of the three times; the PayPal
   link; and THE HOMEWORK: which files to download before the
   hour (they are large), and to have an AI chat open.
5. After the payment, Nir sends the link for the call.
6. THE HOUR, from a printed session sheet:
   0 to 5: What do you want to make? Nir writes it down.
   5 to 45: Make it.
   45 to 55: Once more, alone. Nir watches and says nothing.
   55 to 60: Which Making Of to watch next.
   WHEN STUCK, three sentences are printed on the sheet:
   "Let's read the error together."
   "Let's ask your AI. Paste the error, and say what you were
   trying to do."
   "I do not know. Let's find out."
7. AFTER. The script makes a small copy of the recording, and
   Nir sends it to the customer. One line goes into the private
   log: the date, the studio, whether it worked.

THE PROMISE. Nir promises the hour, not the result. If it did
not work, the customer chooses: a second hour free, or the money
back.

THE RULES (AM-LAW 14). An adult asks and pays. If the learner is
a minor, a parent is in the room. The call is recorded, with the
customer's yes in the email and again aloud at the start.

THE CALL. The EXPERT proposes a call tool, after a search that
Nir carries. It must run in the customer's browser with no
account, share a screen, and last an hour for free. Nir records
with his own recorder, so nothing depends on the tool's
recording.

10.8 HUMANITIES LEVELS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A humanities Level has the same five pieces, the same Guide, the
same film, the same packages and the same line. Ten things
differ.

1. THE LINK is always a story link: the game's world shows
   something, and the record explains it or corrects it.
2. AN IDEA is one sentence that a historian, or a careful
   reader, would sign. It rests on claims of grade standard
   (Part 03, 3.8). Its "Where people slip" lists common wrong
   beliefs.
3. NO GROUND, NO DESCENT. A Level has three to five checkpoints.
   Each idea leans only on earlier checkpoints of the same
   Level. The topic cards come from the school programmes for
   history and literature, fetched as in Part 03, section 3.14.
4. THE BOSS IS A SOURCE. In history: a real document, object or
   building. In literature: a passage from a book in the public
   domain. Its card lives in vault/humanities/sources/ : what it
   is, where it is kept, its date, a photo from Wikimedia
   Commons, and the lines we read, each quoted from a published
   translation.
   Its route is a READING: numbered observations about the
   source, each ending with USES: and an idea. The load limit is
   the same.
5. THE PAGE KINDS.
   SEE tells the idea through one case: one person, one year,
   one place.
   MACHINE becomes THE GAME: what the game shows as the record
   has it, and what the game changed. Each with its claim.
   BOSS: PROBLEM becomes BOSS: SOURCE.
   BOSS: STEP reads one line or one detail of the source with
   one idea.
6. NO NUMBERS FILE. A script checks that every year and every
   number in the text stands in a quote of one of the Level's
   claims.
7. AUDIT A14, SOURCED (text). IN: one page's sentences, and the
   Level's claims. For each sentence: the id of the claim it
   rests on, or NONE. A NONE sends the page back.
8. THE TOY. One new toy type, card-sort: cards are dragged into
   an order, or into two boxes. The last task turns each card
   over and shows its source. The EXPERT builds it once, by the
   steps of Part 05, section 5.13.
9. THE CHALLENGE. PICK: the right answer is an idea's sentence;
   the wrong answers are the wrong beliefs from the idea cards.
   SPOT: a line of the source is shown; which idea does it
   show?
10. YOUR SCENE. The Credits end with an invitation: one short
    scene to make with free AI tools, and the links to the
    Making Ofs. Nothing is sent to us, and nothing is scored.

PICTURES. Photos first. Never a painted picture of a real
person, place or object.

AN EXAMPLE, format only. Game moment: fields under water beside
a great river, in a game set in ancient Egypt. Question: "Why
would anyone farm in a desert?" Ideas: the flood leaves fresh
mud; the height of the flood foretells the harvest; so the
height of the flood was written down every year. Boss: a real
stone on which flood heights were recorded, year by year.

History in which living people fought is Nir's decision, Level
by Level.
