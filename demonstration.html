Demonstration

The Hive is not theory. It has been tested on real hardware, across real networks, with real timestamps, and the results are documented in the public repositories. Every number below can be verified by anyone who clones the code and runs it themselves.

Experiment 1 — Localhost (April 10, 2026): Four separate processes on one laptop. A RajaBee, a DwarfQueen, and two Workers. Each ran its own copy of a 3-billion-parameter AI model. The test question was about space colonization destinations in the Solar System. The RajaBee split it into 3 components, the DwarfQueen further split into 14 total subtasks. All 14 subtasks were processed, combined, and delivered in 166 seconds. The Buzzing calibration system tested both Workers beforehand and assigned proportional work fractions — 88% to the faster Worker, 12% to the slower one.

Experiment 2 — Cross-Machine LAN (April 11, 2026): Two physical machines in two different rooms, connected by a home LAN cable. A laptop with an RTX 5090 GPU and a desktop with an RTX 4070 Ti GPU. The question was about interstellar travel to Alpha Centauri. The system produced 42 components (a splitting bug we later fixed) that cascaded into 406 subtasks distributed across the Workers. Despite the overload, every single one of the 448 work items was processed and combined into a coherent final answer — a comprehensive document covering wormholes, warp drives, light sails, nuclear propulsion, fusion drives, and more. The architecture absorbed a 42× overload without crashing.

Experiment 3 — The Fix (April 11, 2026): We replaced the JSON-based splitter with smart_splitter — a module that lets the AI write in any format (numbered lists, bullet points, paragraphs) and parses whatever comes back. Same hardware, same question, same models. Result: 5 components instead of 42, 15 subtasks instead of 406, 3 minutes instead of 46 minutes. Fifteen times faster, with better quality. The difference wasn't hardware or models — it was one Python module that works with the AI instead of fighting it.

Experiment 4 — Multimedia (April 18, 2026): The same architecture, extended to perceive the physical world. Photos: the Queen produces a low-resolution gestalt, Workers claim image tiles (8 tiles per photo, double-grid offset). Sound: the Queen produces section-level transcriptions from a 2×-compressed copy, Workers claim 5-second temporal slices. Video: the Queen produces frame-level visual gestalt, Workers claim 3-second clips. All three modalities verified end-to-end over real HTTP through the KillerBee website. A 3-minute audio clip produced 11,639 characters of coherent transcription. The architecture that handles text handles sight and sound with no modification.
