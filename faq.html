FAQ

Is the Hive actually working software? Yes. It has been tested across two operating systems (Windows and Linux), in three configurations (localhost, LAN, and real internet through Cloudflare Tunnel), with real AI models processing real jobs. On March 22, 2026, two Windows machines on a home network ran a distributed AI job together for the first time. On March 25, the same system ran through the real internet in 29 seconds. On April 10-11, 2026, hierarchical hives with RajaBees, DwarfQueens, and Workers were tested across two physical machines. On April 18, the multimedia pipeline was proven end-to-end: photos, sound, and video. Every experiment is documented with commit hashes anyone can verify.

What hardware do I need? For a Worker: any computer made in the last 5-6 years. A graphics card helps but isn't required. For a Queen: a more powerful machine — a desktop with a good GPU, or a cloud VPS. The entire system runs on consumer hardware. The AI models (llama, DeepSeek, Qwen) are free to download and run locally.

Does the system require an internet connection? Only for the Public Mode, where Workers connect to a marketplace website. In Private Mode, the system runs entirely on an organization's internal network — no internet connection required at all. Data never leaves the building.

How is this different from ChatGPT or Claude? Cloud AI runs on someone else's computers. You send your data to their servers, they process it, they charge you. The Hive runs on your own hardware. Your data stays with you. No subscription, no API key, no terms of service, no usage limits. The AI models are open-weight — you can inspect them, fine-tune them, and run them however you want.

Is this an AI agent? Will it take over my computer? No. The Worker AI does exactly one thing: receives a specific, clearly defined sub-task, processes it, and returns the result. It does not browse the internet, send emails, open programs, or make autonomous decisions. All coordination is done by the Queen, and the Queen only does what the Beekeeper asked for. This is task processing, not autonomous agency.

How is the alignment problem handled? By architecture, not by training. The Hive decomposes every job into isolated sub-tasks handled by separate Workers. A Worker sees only its tiny piece — it has no awareness of the larger task, no shared state with other Workers, and no ability to coordinate with them. The RajaBee at the top is constrained in both agency (no web browsing, no self-scaffolding, no autonomous tools) and hardware (consumer-class, not a datacenter cluster). Alignment becomes an architectural property — verifiable in the code, not dependent on hoping the training worked.

How was this built? The entire platform was built by one person — Nir Strulovitz — in seven days using AI-assisted coding ("vibe coding"). Approximately 4,000 lines of Python. Free AI models. Consumer hardware. No cloud, no subscription, no funding. The book "The Distributed AI Revolution" documents the entire process, day by day, commit by commit.
