A wide evening illustration of a small glass-walled capsule store — a compact prefabricated box about nine square metres, glowing with light — standing on a Hong Kong harbourfront promenade with the city skyline and water behind it. Inside, a single humanoid robot the height of a person, with a vaguely human upper body and conspicuously long arms, stands behind the counter handing a canned drink to a customer; behind it, shelves hold remote-controlled toy taxis and squishy toys shaped like an egg waffle and a pineapple bun. A speech bubble above the robot shows the same greeting in English, Mandarin and Cantonese. Outside, a mixed crowd reacts: a father and his daughter film the robot on a phone, a woman watches with folded arms and a skeptical eyebrow, and a small child claps as the robot begins a little dance. There is no human staff anywhere in the store. In the far background, silhouettes of identical capsules loaded on a delivery truck hint at many more rolling out to other cities.
