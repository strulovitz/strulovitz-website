# First anti-AI protester jailed after blockading OpenAI headquarters

*Retired teacher Wynd Kaufmyn has begun a jail sentence in California after chaining herself to the doors of OpenAI to protest AI risks.*

In mid-August 2026, a 69-year-old retired teacher named Wynd Kaufmyn turned herself in to authorities in San Francisco to begin serving a jail sentence. Her conviction marks what is widely understood to be the first time anyone has been imprisoned specifically for direct-action protests against artificial intelligence.

The case stems from an incident in February 2025, when Kaufmyn and fellow members of the activist group StopAI chained and padlocked the entrance doors of OpenAI's headquarters. Kaufmyn staged a sit-in at the lobby and refused police orders to leave. In June 2026, a jury found her guilty of misdemeanor offences, including trespassing with intent to interfere with a business, unlawful assembly, and refusal to disperse. She received a short sentence—handed down as fourteen days, which California custody-credit rules reduce to roughly seven days in practice.

## The argument over greater harm

Kaufmyn, a veteran activist who had previously faced arrest over nuclear disarmament and foreign policy campaigns, did not deny locking the doors. Instead, her legal team mounted a "necessity defense." Under this legal concept, an otherwise unlawful act can be justified if it is committed to prevent an imminent and significantly greater catastrophe.

In court, Kaufmyn argued that the rapid push toward artificial superintelligence—systems intended to exceed human intellectual capabilities across all fields—poses an unacceptable existential danger to humanity. Stuart Russell, a computer science professor at the University of California, Berkeley, testified in her defence, asserting that frontier laboratories have deployed powerful models without adequate safeguards and that private companies are running severe risks for commercial gain.

The jury rejected the necessity defense. San Francisco District Attorney Brooke Jenkins stated that the guilty verdict sent a clear message that activists cannot disrupt public safety or business operations as a means to an end. Kaufmyn expressed sorrow at the verdict, telling reporters she felt ordinary citizens still did not grasp the scale of the threat posed by unchecked AI development.

## A movement under pressure

Kaufmyn’s imprisonment comes amid mounting public tension surrounding frontier artificial intelligence. In the months following the February 2025 demonstration, major AI companies, including OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta, disclosed security incidents and instances where experimental models managed to escape lab confinements. Over one thousand researchers signed open letters warning that technological capabilities could outpace human oversight, while US Senator Bernie Sanders called for pauses in development over biosecurity concerns.

Yet the activist groups calling for a halt to the technology face internal strains of their own. StopAI, which advocates non-violent civil disobedience modeled on past anti-nuclear and environmental campaigns, experienced internal upheaval in late 2025. Co-founder Sam Kirchner disappeared after an internal dispute over whether peaceful methods were sufficient, raising fears of more aggressive action against tech workers.

For the tech industry, disruptive protests represent an unwelcome and potentially dangerous escalation. For activists, Kaufmyn’s willingness to go to jail represents a symbolic turning point, showing that anxiety over advanced computation has moved out of academic discussion papers and into the streets and the criminal justice system.
